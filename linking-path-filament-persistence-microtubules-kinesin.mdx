Inside living cells, microtubules and kinesin motors carry material from place to place. The same system can be rebuilt outside the cell as molecular shuttles: kinesin motors fixed to a surface push microtubules, which can carry cargo. How well these shuttles work depends on their paths. A shuttle that wanders is harder to steer to a target.

The straightness of a path is measured by the path persistence length. Theory predicted that it should equal the filament persistence length, which measures how stiff the microtubule is in bending. Experiments, however, have found a significant gap between the two. Without an explanation, a designer cannot use the stiffness of the microtubule to predict how its shuttle will move.

## What we did

We used computer simulation to find where the gap comes from. We simulated molecular shuttles moving under external forces and measured both the path persistence length and the filament persistence length. We then looked closely at how the microtubules bent as they moved.

## What we found

- The simulation reproduced the gap between path and filament persistence lengths, as seen in experiments.
- The part of the microtubule bent by the external force was extended further than the theory had assumed.
- When this extended length is taken into account, the gap between the two persistence lengths is explained.

## Why it matters

For anyone designing a molecular shuttle device, the path persistence length is what matters in practice, because it describes where the shuttle actually goes. This study connects it back to a property of the filament that can be measured, once the true length of the bent section is included. That gives a better basis for controlling shuttle movement, for example when an external force is used to guide the shuttles.

## Limits

This summary is written from the published abstract. The results come from simulation under external forces. The abstract does not give the force values used, the size of the gap in numbers, or whether the same explanation holds for shuttles moving without an external force.
